# INC-009 - Disruption notifications taking four hours to deliver

**Date:** 2026-02-04
**Duration:** 4h
**Severity:** SEV-2
**Author:** Notifications team

## Summary

During a weather disruption affecting eleven flights, rebooking notifications
took nearly four hours to reach all affected passengers. Many arrived after the
replacement flights had already departed.

## Timeline

- **05:30** - Eleven flights cancelled. Rebooking job triggered.
- **05:31** - Notification job starts.
- **09:20** - Final notification sent.

## Impact

Roughly 1,700 passengers. An estimated 200 missed their replacement flight
because they were not informed in time. Significant support volume and several
compensation claims.

## What went wrong

The notification job iterates over the affected passenger list and awaits the
delivery call for each one in turn, one passenger at a time. Each delivery
takes between two and nine seconds because it fetches the passenger's contact
preferences and renders a template.

The job was written for the common case of a single delayed flight with a few
dozen passengers, where sequential delivery finishes in under two minutes.
Nothing in the code limits or parallelises the work, and nothing scales with
the size of the disruption.

The job did not fail. It just kept running, so no alert fired.

## Resolution

Delivery now runs in bounded batches with a concurrency limit, and the job
reports progress so a stalled run is visible.

## Action items

- Any loop that awaits per item needs an explicit concurrency policy.
- Alert on job duration, not only on job failure.
