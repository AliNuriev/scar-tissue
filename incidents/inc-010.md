# INC-010 - Price watch workers degrading over 48 hours

**Date:** 2026-06-27
**Duration:** 48h (progressive)
**Severity:** SEV-3
**Author:** Pricing team

## Summary

The price watch service became progressively slower over two days and had to be
restarted every night. The nightly restart was in the runbook for three weeks
before anyone questioned it.

## Timeline

- **June 3** - Price watch feature ships.
- **June 6** - First nightly restart added to the runbook as a workaround.
- **June 27** - New engineer asks why the restart is there.
- **June 27, 15:00** - Root cause found.

## Impact

No customer-visible outage. Alert latency on price drops grew from under a
minute to over twenty. Three weeks of an unexplained restart being treated as
normal operations.

## What went wrong

When a customer creates a price watch, the service starts an interval that
polls the fare for that route. When the watch is deleted or expires, the record
is removed from the database but the interval is never cleared.

Every deleted watch therefore left a live timer behind, still polling, still
holding its closure in memory. Over a day the process accumulated several
thousand orphaned timers.

The restart cleared them, which is why the workaround appeared to work and why
nobody looked further.

## Resolution

Timer handles are now tracked alongside the watch record and cleared in the
same code path that removes it, inside a `finally` so that a failed deletion
still stops the timer.

## Action items

- Anything started must have an owner responsible for stopping it.
- A recurring restart in a runbook is an open incident.
